How a morning goes

For anyone whose morning "takes 30 minutes" and actually takes 50. Morrow times the real thing and plans around it.

  1. Set your leave time tonight

    Morrow takes your routine and a 5-minute buffer away from it and shows the wake-up time, with the reason in one line.

  2. The alarm rings

    Morrow sets a system alarm on your iPhone. The snooze button says what a snooze costs, like "Snooze 9 · no coffee."

  3. One step at a time

    Each step counts down on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, with a line that says if you're on track, behind or ahead. Spoken cues tell you when to move on, so you don't have to watch the screen.

  4. Out the door

    The last step is a door check for keys, wallet, or whatever you tend to forget. Then a receipt shows each step and the time you left.

It learns how long things really take

Morrow records how long each step takes and moves tomorrow's plan to match. After a few mornings, the guess you typed in is replaced by your real time.

Learning moves the alarm by at most 5 minutes a day, and Morrow always tells you why. If you fall behind, "I'll be late" writes the text for you to send. With Plus, Morrow can also skip a nice-to-have step or shorten the flexible ones.

Meet Mo

Mo is a small sun who gets ready with you. Mo brushes when you brush, puts on a scarf when you get dressed and waves when you leave. Miss a morning and Mo just slept in.

You pick a goal of 3 to 5 weekdays. One bad morning doesn't reset anything, and reaching the goal earns Mo something new to wear.
